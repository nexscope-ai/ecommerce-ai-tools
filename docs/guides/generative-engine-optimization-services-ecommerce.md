# Generative Engine Optimization Services for Ecommerce: What to Ask For

Before hiring a generative engine optimization service, pick the outcome you need to inspect. A one-time review answers what sampled AI responses say about a product today. Monitoring shows how answers change across repeat tests. Implementation changes your site or product data. Price and responsibilities depend on which job you are buying.

## Compare the deliverables

| Scope | Useful deliverable | Who acts on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-product review | Dated example answers, matched product and sources, gaps to investigate | Merchant or ecommerce team |
| Ongoing monitoring | Repeatable prompt set, schedule and history of mentions and citations | Marketing or analytics team |
| Implementation | Approved changes to product facts, markup, content or feeds, with validation | Site owner or delivery partner |

In a sample report, look for the exact question, product variant, target market, response and cited page when available. Ask how the provider handles a result that mentions the brand but recommends another model. A single “visibility score” can hide that distinction.

## Define what a meaningful finding looks like

Suppose your store sells a USB-C desk lamp and an answer recommends the older model. Record which model the answer named, where the claim came from and which product page confirms the current specs. If the page never states whether the USB-C port powers the lamp or only charges a phone, the next task is to verify that fact and publish it clearly. If the source is an old review, record the date and where the current information is available. Those cases call for different fixes.

For Google’s AI features, start with the same technical eligibility and content fundamentals that apply to Search. Use the platform’s own guidance when scoping work for other AI surfaces. [Google Search Central guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
