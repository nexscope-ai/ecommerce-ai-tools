# How to research an Amazon product idea with Nexscope Keyword Researcher

**Goal:** Start with one product idea, find related Amazon search terms, compare demand and competition signals, and open product samples before deciding what to investigate next. This tutorial uses `dog grooming kit` as an **example input**, not as a recommendation or a claim about its current market performance.

## 1. Search in the right marketplace

Open [Nexscope Amazon Keyword Researcher](https://www.nexscope.ai/seller/amazon-keyword-researcher?co-from=learn&utm_source=learn.nexscope.ai&utm_medium=referral) and sign in when prompted. Choose a **Marketplace**—US, UK, DE, JP, or CA—before entering a term in **Keyword**. For a first run, choose **US**, enter `dog grooming kit`, and select **Search**.

The page may take a few minutes to fetch Amazon data. Watch the status message while the search runs. Do not treat the initial loading values as results. If no keywords are found, the page suggests trying a broader term, removing model numbers or narrow attributes, or switching marketplace.

## 2. Read the overview as a screening tool

When the search completes, start with the four overview cards:

- **Keywords Found** counts the related search terms returned for your input.
- **Est. Monthly Revenue** summarizes estimated revenue across the enriched keyword data. It is a demand signal, **not a forecast of your sales**.
- **Avg. Top 3 Share** indicates how much of the sampled sales the leading three products capture. A higher share can signal a concentrated competitive set.
- **Avg. New Launch Share** indicates the sampled sales share of newer listings. It may suggest whether recent entrants appear in the market; it cannot predict that your launch will succeed.

These are market and product-sample estimates. Keep the selected country, date, and original keyword with any numbers you save. If a metric is still refreshing or unavailable, leave it blank in your notes rather than filling in a zero.

## 3. Compare related terms, then inspect products

In **Keyword Opportunities**, review the rows for **Amazon Rank**, **Units**, **Price**, **Revenue**, **Rating**, **Top 3 Share**, **New Launch**, and **Search Rank Trend** where data is available. Ask three questions of each candidate term: Does it match the product you could actually sell? Is the price range compatible with your costs? Are the leading products so dominant that you need a clear differentiation plan?

Choose a term worth investigating and use its **View Products** action. Inspect the returned product sample instead of deciding from the keyword table alone. Note product format, price, rating, and any meaningful variation in the offers. The sample is a starting point for comparison, not a complete list of every Amazon seller.

## 4. Save a small decision brief

Use **Export CSV** to keep the keyword rows. Make a shortlist of two or three terms and record:

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Search context | Seed term, marketplace, run date |
| Candidate | Related keyword and why it fits your proposed product |
| Evidence | Available estimated units, revenue, price, ratings, concentration and newer-listing signals |
| Follow-up | Product sample to inspect, customer question to research, or cost to verify |

You can reopen a recent search from the **Recent searches** row if it is available. If product metrics could not finish refreshing—for example, because of an account-credit issue—the keyword opportunities may still be visible. Label the missing product metrics rather than treating them as unfavorable results.

**What this gives you:** a traceable shortlist for further product research, not a “winning product” guarantee.
