## Which looks better in user comparisons?

There is no defensible universal winner in the public examples reviewed here. A model can follow a complex relationship more closely while producing a style a viewer likes less. Naturalness, prompt adherence and subject identity are separate judgments.

In a [February 28, 2026 same-prompt comparison](https://www.reddit.com/r/Bard/comments/1rgyuyf/nano_banana_vs_nano_banana_pro_vs_nano_banana_2/), the author preferred Nano Banana 2 for depicting a painter and her self-portrait consistently. Comments disagreed over the artificial look and aesthetic preference. A [separate fashion comparison](https://www.reddit.com/r/GenAIGallery/comments/1rha28f/testing_nano_banana_pro_nano_banana_2_seedream_v5/) included a preference for Pro's naturalness, but also criticism that the outputs looked overly perfect. Those examples used other hosts; they are not measurements of these Nexscope routes.

Use that disagreement to design your test rather than averaging it into a rating. For a cosmetic bottle, preservation of the cap and printed label may matter more than dramatic lighting. For an illustrated campaign concept, style may carry more weight.

## Is Nano Banana 2 cheaper or faster than Pro?

The lower published route estimate makes Nano Banana 2 a reasonable candidate for a cost-conscious pilot. It does not prove a matched-request saving or a speed advantage. This article has not timed the two routes or measured rejection rates.

Use **total credits spent ÷ approved images** to compare usable-output cost. Include rejected attempts in the numerator. For example, if a hypothetical test spends 600 credits and approves two images, its cost is 300 credits per approved image. That arithmetic is an illustration, not a result for either model.

When recording time, start from submission and stop when a completed image is available. Compare the same output count and settings; do not compare a single 1K image with a multi-image 4K request. Keep queue time separate where it is visible.

## How should you compare them for product photography?

Use the same permitted product reference and brief, then score product fidelity before visual polish. This is a suggested evaluation protocol, not a claim that either model has passed it.

1. Pick one real product photo with readable packaging and clear edges. Mark the details that must not change.
2. Start both routes at one output, the same resolution and aspect ratio. Record anything that cannot be matched.
3. Ask for one controlled change, such as a neutral lifestyle background. Avoid changing the product and scene simultaneously.
4. Compare the actual item, label text, proportions, color and included accessories. Reject factual changes.
5. Inspect lighting, framing and whether the requested scene was followed.
6. Save every attempt, elapsed time and charged credits. Repeat before declaring a preference.

| Check | Passing evidence | Reason to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product identity | Recognisable shape and packaging match | Changed cap, silhouette or logo |
| Text and claims | Approved label and truthful presentation | Invented text or implied features |
| Scene fit | Requested context without hiding the item | Extra accessories or misleading use |
| Revision locality | Requested edit without unrelated changes | Product changes during background revision |

## Which model should you try first?

Start with Nano Banana 2 if the published estimate fits your budget and you need to explore a reference-led brief. Add Pro to the same pilot when naturalness or a polished campaign look is important. These are testing priorities, not measured performance rankings.

If neither preserves the item, keep the original product photography and use generated imagery only where it will not misrepresent the product.
