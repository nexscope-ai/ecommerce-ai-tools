# How to compare Amazon Best Sellers and New Launches with Nexscope

**Goal:** Use one marketplace and subcategory to compare established products with newer listings, save a few ASINs for follow-up research, and avoid mistaking estimated sales for your own sales forecast.

## 1. Choose the market and product group

Open [Nexscope Amazon Product Rankings](https://www.nexscope.ai/seller/amazon-product-rankings?co-from=learn&utm_source=learn.nexscope.ai&utm_medium=referral) and sign in if the workspace asks you to. In **Marketplace**, choose the country you plan to sell in: US, UK, DE, JP, or CA. Select a **Core Category**, then a **Subcategory**. Make these choices before drawing a conclusion; a ranking in one subcategory or country cannot be generalized to another.

The page loads a list after a subcategory is selected. If it has not finished loading, wait for the status line under **Product Information** rather than reading an empty table as “no competition.”

## 2. Compare two different ranking views

Start on **Best Sellers**. The on-page help defines this view as ranked by **recent 30-day estimated sales**. Look for common product formats, prices, brands, and ratings. Then switch to **New Launches**. The help describes products listed within six months, ranked by recency and early sales. This view can reveal recent entrants worth examining, but it does not prove that a new product will repeat their result.

Use the same marketplace and subcategory for both views. For each, record the date and the ranking type. A “Best Sellers” position here is the tool's estimated ranking view; do not relabel it an official Amazon Best Sellers Rank or claim that the monthly units are your future orders.

## 3. Read each row as a product-research lead

The **Product Information** table shows fields such as **Rank**, **Product Name**, **Monthly Units**, **Monthly Revenue**, **Sales Trend**, **Price**, **Price Trend**, **ASIN**, **Brand**, and **Listed Date** when the data is available. Take one established product and one newer listing that serve the same buyer need. Compare:

1. Is the price difference explained by size, materials, packaging, or a different bundle?
2. Does the sales trend look stable enough to justify another research step, or does it need a longer history check?
3. Is the newer listing actually comparable, or is it targeting a different customer?

Open the relevant product pages separately and verify attributes, reviews, fees, and restrictions. The ranking table helps you choose what to inspect; it does not perform supplier due diligence or calculate your margin.

## 4. Export only what you will investigate

Select **Export CSV** after setting the marketplace, ranking view, and subcategory. In a working sheet, keep the exported ASIN, name, price, available estimates, and your own research notes. Add two columns that the ranking cannot answer by itself: **product differentiation to verify** and **cost or compliance question**.

If you want a compact first exercise, shortlist three ASINs: a leading established product, a mid-list alternative, and a newer listing. Compare their actual listings and customer feedback before deciding whether to source anything. Nexscope's ranking views can shorten the discovery step, but estimates and rankings are not a launch recommendation.
